The company marches in from both wings into two blocks, looking up at him. Lafayette bursts in up centre through the company on the shout (17.6), raps on the deck and is centre in the ring of beams by 31.3 (cue 562); the company rings the turntable facing him, then turns it 120 degrees as the rap travels. Washington comes through D-UC to centre for 59.2 (his special); the company forms an upstage arc. For the chant they make two blocks, an arc round Washington, who sings his anthem in his centre special (Viper 93), and a V for the held note. Hamilton enters DSR at the end and faces Washington for the button. The company stays in the upstage dark. 1-19 History Has Its Eyes On You (cue-led, mostly invented): Washington stays in his special (0, 1.96) all song, Hamilton DS of him, SR. The company steps out of the dark for each chorus and is a full arc for the gospel finish; in the transition they form the Yorktown line (z -1.4). Mulligan slips out (he is behind enemy lines until 1-20 109.9). 1-20 Yorktown (partly sourced): Hamilton on the box near the lip. The line advances, falls back, then surges for the company Shot reprise. Lafayette and Hamilton have the farewell exchange in the centre pool of preset 402 (0, 1.85), the line fallen back behind them. On the first title phrase the line becomes a turning ring, then recedes; Hamilton is alone at the lip, then steps into his DS special (61.2). The night attack: six soldiers climb to the galleries (two each on the SR arm, the SLG and the USG) for the gallery specials at 85.4, and the six deck soldiers and Hamilton each stand in one of the night pools (Hamilton DSC), advancing slowly inside them. Laurens keeps his SL pool (Viper 13) for the friends' fortunes and Lafayette takes the DR pool (100.1). Mulligan bursts in from D-UC (109.9) and the deck soldiers rally round him while the gallery soldiers come down. The drill is a 3x4 block. The battle: two ranked lines that charge, the two revolves walked against each other, a freeze into single pools with a slow-motion drift on the cold turn (141.7), then the lines re-form and charge to the lip. ens5 goes out D-UC and comes back through it as the redcoat with the white flag, in the cool UC special on the deck (0, -1.6) (155). Four soldiers and Hamilton take the warm pools watching him, then stand in a cold arc with Hamilton in the one warm pool. Washington comes through D-UC for the terms (179.8). The celebration is full stage. In The World Turned Upside Down, nine soldiers are on the outer ring and four on the inner, and the two rings counter-rotate (walked) at 204.2 and reverse at 217.1. At the final tableau each soldier steps into his own pool (twelve pools; two of the company stay in the dark). Everyone goes off up/mid wings in the blackout. 1-21 What Comes Next? (sourced as to place): the King comes on DSL in the blackout to his lip special. He makes small moves and leaves upstage through D-UC at the transition. 1-22 Dear Theodosia (sourced from the cue research frame): Burr in his SL pool and Hamilton in his SR pool. Each moves within his own pool on his verse; they stay in their separate pools for the duet. They stay on in the dark for Non-Stop. 1-23 Non-Stop (cue-led, partly invented): Burr narrates, then joins Weeks at the table as co-counsel. The jury of six and the judge sit along the desk (preset 615: a row of narrow pools at z -0.6 across the stage), Levi Weeks is at the table at C, and two onlookers stand SL; Hamilton addresses the jury from his DS special. The company makes an arc at the first chorus and a turning outer ring at the hook. Hamilton steps into his DS special (X4 44) for his brag. At the Convention the women leave and six delegates make an upstage arc. Hamilton goes to centre on 633 (Move to center); the men ring him (635) and close in (636). For Burr/Men (638) Burr is DSC with the men in a wedge behind. Midnight at Burr's is DR (766 AH Burr DR), and the two move into the warm-white special (-1.25, 2.79) on 642. For Burr's reprise he is alone at C and Hamilton exits. The company watches from the three galleries. Angelica in her SL key, up left (3.6, -0.4) (207.3), Hamilton DS (225.4), Eliza SR (231.4). Hamilton writes at his desk in his DS key (-1.0, 2.65) while Burr counts DR, and the watchers come down onto the ring and turn it. Washington makes his offer in his deck special by Hamilton (304.3, through D-UC), then climbs the SL stair to the SL gallery (4.9, -1.5) for the finale (BroadwayWorld forum: upper balcony, stage left). The sisters are in their pools DSR and DSL (327.1), then in the finale specials at (+-3.3, 1.6) (332.2), with Hamilton below at (0.05, 2.3). For the counterpoint the company turns 108 degrees on the inner disc (352.4-364.8) and steps out, opening at 6 o'clock for Hamilton's walk to DC (-0.4, 3.5) for his name (374).
