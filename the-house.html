The room is the Palace Theatre, 1564 Broadway — Kirchhoff & Rose, 1913. Plain plaster walls over a mahogany wainscot, two balconies sloping down toward the stage, a coved ceiling on a gilded cornice and a 14 ft bronze chandelier hanging out of the dome. No pit — the apron bows out into the house across the whole width of the stage.

You are the technician. The board is divided into eight sections — front wash, stage wash, back light, cyc, moving heads, followspot, house and work light. Each one has a brightness fader and a colour; you never touch a single lamp. Fourteen counterweight linesets fly scenery in and out of a 25 m loft, and if you unlock one you can haul it by hand from the deck.

Behind the rear wall is the grand foyer — box office, bar, poster frames, a staircase up to the mezzanine gallery and the street doors. The doors between the lobby and the seats really swing: look at one and press E, or use DOORS OPEN / DOORS SHUT.

Behind the stage is the crossover, then the loading dock, and beyond that the scene shop — a separate shed where you do the work with your hands. Cut timber on the saw, frame it up in the jig, cut and stretch cloth over it, dip a brush in a pot and paint the thing, then carry it back and put it on the stage. Nothing is done from a menu.

Bring every section to zero and the house goes properly black.

Build a set on the deck, hang goods on the rail, write a look, record it as a cue, then run the stack with GO. Or drop into the CODE tab and program the whole show as a timed script.

Press H again to close.
